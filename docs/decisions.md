# Decisions

For the app after Epic 1, use the [product PRD](product_prd.md). The owner
selected all-Italy coverage as the goal, and D24 covers all 20 regions in the
first version. The owner accepted clearly labelled experimental
percentages at launch. Field validation is not a release condition. The
completed investigation and its historical decisions below remain evidence.
Their earlier product suggestions do not override the later interview.

## Assumptions

Each assumption comes from section 2 of the PRD. A final status is one of
VALIDATED, REJECTED or INCONCLUSIVE. The evidence link must point to a report
or run record in this repository. Until an experiment finishes, the status is
NOT TESTED.

| ID | Assumption | Experiment and issue | Status | Reason | Evidence link |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Enough trustworthy, dated, located porcini finds exist. | P1, #3 | INCONCLUSIVE | Switzerland and Austria have 4,747 reliable finds, enough to test weather. Tuscany and the Apennines have 85, too few on their own. Weekly Google searches for "porcini" in Tuscany peak within 14 days of the middle find date in 5 of 7 years, against 2 of 7 for a fixed calendar, so they can check season timing during development but cannot stand in for finds or feed the app. | [P1 report](../reports/p1_observations.md), [run record](../reports/runs/p1_observations/20261010T181005Z-696b43.json), [P1c report](../reports/p1c_flush_signs.md), [P1c run record](../reports/runs/p1c_flush_signs/20261011T080539Z-692d26.json) |
| A2 | Tree and habitat data help at regional level. | P2, #4 and #5 | VALIDATED | A 504-square sample grid can be rebuilt with one command. Terrain, rock type, forest cover and broadleaf or conifer cover at least 98 percent of squares at detail finer than 500 m. The Tuscany forest inventory names beech, chestnut and oak woods at one point every 400 m, but dates from 1985 to 1993 and stops at the regional border. Soil from SoilGrids is too uncertain to separate squares. | [P2 report](../reports/p2_habitat.md), [run record](../reports/runs/p2_habitat/20261010T192011Z-c4f95b.json) |
| A3 | Past weather is enough to judge conditions today. | P3a, #6 | VALIDATED | One command rebuilds 30 days of rain, temperature, evaporation and soil values before 300 finds, with source and cost, at about 3.2 free Open-Meteo calls per find. Reconstructed past weather runs 5 to 6 days behind, so on a real morning the last 6 days come from weather model values, which exist from 2017. Models disagree by up to 100 mm on recent rain, and rain detail is about 25 km. | [P3 report](../reports/p3_weather.md), [run record](../reports/runs/p3_weather/20261010T195440Z-a38289.json) |
| A4 | A forecast 7 days ahead can be tested without knowing the outcome. | P3b, #7 | VALIDATED | The Open-Meteo Single Runs API keeps every ECMWF IFS 9 km run since 14 March 2024 with its run time. That covers 765 of 4,832 finds, 36 of them in Tuscany, all from 2024 onwards. Older finds have no stored forecast and cannot be tested this way. On 100 finds, rain 1 to 3 days ahead follows the rain that fell closely, and 6 to 7 days ahead the link is weak. | [P3 report, forecasts published in the past](../reports/p3_weather.md#forecasts-published-in-the-past), [run record](../reports/runs/p3b_forecasts/20261011T070425Z-d61547.json) |
| A5 | Weather and habitat signals beat a simple guess. | P4, #8 and #9 | INCONCLUSIVE | Rain of the 2 to 4 weeks before a day points to when porcini appear: in a German forest counted day by day, 95 percent of porcini days had at least 30 mm of rain from 30 to 8 days before, against 78 percent of other days. Weather cannot pick a place inside a 25 km cell. The only model that beat the simple guesses on places learned habitat from finds, mainly flat ground near paths, and it failed on the final set of 2025 and 2026, so it showed where people walk. On that final set, scored once, rain of the last 30 days put 41 percent of find days in the top fifth against 21 for a random ranking, and the weather rules from published studies reached 27 percent. | [P4b report](../reports/p4_benchmark.md), [final run record](../reports/runs/p4b_benchmark/20261011T105039Z-5950ac.json), [Bielefeld run record](../reports/runs/p4b_bielefeld/20261011T101125Z-c3dfaf.json) |
| A6 | Precomputed results can be served efficiently on a phone. | P5, #10 | VALIDATED | Serving is cheap: a full phone screen of squares is 7.4 KB compressed at the starting zoom and 27 KB at the widest allowed view of 1,086 squares, and the API uses 35 to 42 MB of memory and answers in at most 11 ms. In a Chromium phone window the page is usable in 0.9 to 3.5 seconds and stays smooth over 25 moves of the map. On an iPhone 15 with Safari the page opened quickly but got slower with each move of the map and then froze, while the API answered every request, so that is a bug in the prototype page, not a limit of serving the data. Data is made up. | [P5 report](../reports/p5_phone_map.md), [run record](../reports/runs/p5_phone_map/20261011T111704Z-e8170c.json) |
| A7 | Licences and running costs allow a free small service. | P6, #11 | VALIDATED | The backup and server move test of #12 was left out of Epic 1 by the project owner. No source used in Epic 1 stops a free app. The free Open-Meteo plan fits a free app with no ads and no subscriptions, at about 1,036 calls a day against 10,000 allowed. GADM outlines cannot ship in the app and need replacing, finds and their photos stay out of the app, and forest inventory values keep CC BY-SA 4.0. On 11 October 2026 the cheapest server with 4 GB of memory that could be ordered cost 7.20 euro a month with VAT on a 24-month contract and 9.74 euro month by month, at Netcup. Cheaper OVH and Hetzner servers, 4.65 to 8.65 euro, were sold out. 100 users add about 72,000 requests and under 1 GB of data a month, which does not change the cost. If the app turns commercial, Open-Meteo costs at least 29 euro a month. | [P6 report](../reports/p6_cost_licence_portability.md), [licence table](../reports/p6_licences.csv), [run record](../reports/runs/p6_costs/20261011T142322Z-4fe9ea.json) |

## Decision log

| ID | Date | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | 2026-10-10 | Use 500 m squares in EPSG:3035 with IDs like `500m_E4321500_N2345000`. | One fixed grid in metres keeps squares equal in area and lets every dataset join on the same ID. |
| D2 | 2026-10-10 | A day runs from midnight to midnight Italian time, Europe/Rome. | Finds, weather and users all follow Italian local days, so one rule avoids shifted dates. |
| D3 | 2026-10-10 | Study the four porcini species as one group: *Boletus edulis* GBIF 5954958, *B. aereus* GBIF 8733688, *B. reticulatus* GBIF 5954691 and *B. pinophilus* GBIF 5954949. *B. aestivalis* is a GBIF synonym of *B. reticulatus* and is included through that ID. iNaturalist taxon IDs are 48701, 333772, 350216 and 335942. | GBIF count queries on this date found 15 *B. edulis* records in all of Tuscany and about 100 for the four species together. One species alone is too few to test anything. |
| D4 | 2026-10-10 | Use two areas. The home area is Tuscany, GADM ITA.16_1, plus Emilia-Romagna, GADM ITA.6_1, south of latitude 44.30 N to cover the Apennine side. Pistoia province, GADM ITA.16.8_1, is reported on its own. The comparison area is Switzerland plus Austria. | The home area has about 130 to 160 separate finds, which may be too few. Switzerland and Austria have about 8,900 records since 2000 in mountain forests closer to the Apennines than northern Europe. A result there shows whether weather predicts finds at all. Tuscany shows whether it holds where the app will be used. |
| D5 | 2026-10-11 | P4 follows the test rules in `docs/test_rules.md`: one test case is a 500 m square on one day, comparison area blocks of 100 km with a 25 km gap, test years 2022 to 2024, final years 2025 and 2026, and success judged by the share of finds in the top 20 percent of forest squares on the same day. | The rules are fixed before any prediction is scored, so no threshold can be bent to fit a result. Finds only show where people found porcini, so only rankings are fair. |
| D6 | 2026-10-11 | P4b scores only cases from 2017 on: tuning 2017 to 2021 with 392 cases, test 2022 to 2024 with 737, final 2025 and 2026 with 656. Gradient-boosted trees are not scored. The rain guess uses the 7 days that end the day before. Weather comes from ERA5 hourly time series of the Copernicus data store, one request per 25 km cell, summed into Italian days. | Set before any prediction was scored. The weather of a real morning exists only from 2017, see A3. Every comparison point needs weather, and each 25 km cell costs one request of about 2 minutes, so ten years keep it to 380 short requests. 392 tuning cases from 5 seasons suit hand-written rules and logistic regression, not a more flexible model. |
| D7 | 2026-10-11 | Add a timing test to P4b: rank each find day against the other days from 45 days before to 45 days after, in the same square, with checks T1 and T2 in `docs/test_rules.md`. | Added after the first test scores and before any timing score. The test that ranks places on one day cannot show whether rain decides when porcini appear, because every find day already follows rain and rain comes in 25 km cells. |
| D8 | 2026-10-11 | Replace the hand-written rules with rules from published knowledge: at least 30 mm of rain from 30 to 8 days before, a cooling of at least 1 degree over the last week, a 14-day mean of 10 to 15 degrees, and altitude from 200 to 2,000 m. Sources are listed in `experiments/p4b_benchmark/config.toml`. | The first rules took the altitude band from tuning finds, which follows where people search. Test scores of the first rules had been seen, so the report shows both versions. The final set has not been used. |
| D9 | 2026-10-11 | Top 10 and top 20 percent scores count a tie between the find and other places as a random tie break. | A hand-written rule gives only a few distinct values, so a find often ties with hundreds of places. Counting a tie as half the span put such finds just outside the top 20 percent every time, so a rule could score 0 even when it was right. A rule that gives every place the same value now scores like a random ranking. Found after seeing test scores. Continuous models are unchanged. |
| D10 | 2026-10-11 | Move every ERA5 temperature from the height of its 25 km cell to the height of the square, at 6.5 degrees per 1,000 m, using the ERA5 ground height of each cell. Add a season rule from 15 June to 15 November. Rules come from published studies only. Finds check the rules roughly but never set their thresholds. | ERA5 gives one temperature for a whole cell of mountains and valleys, so a temperature rule misjudged slopes far above or below the cell. The user decided that rules must follow published knowledge of porcini, because finds only show where people looked. |
| D11 | 2026-10-11 | Epic 1 ends with PIVOT: build a free phone map of conditions for porcini in Tuscany and the Apennines, based on rules from published studies, and never show a chance of finding porcini. | Weather points to when porcini appear, but nothing tested points to where inside a 25 km weather cell, and the home area has 85 reliable finds. Licences, costs and serving allow a free conditions map. See `reports/EPIC1_VALIDATION_REPORT.md`. |
| D12 | 2026-10-11 | The app is web first. It must work on a phone, and it must also have a full desktop layout, which may offer more than the phone version. This replaces "phone map" in D11. | Decided by the project owner after reading the Epic 1 report. P5 tested only a phone-sized page, so the desktop layout has not been tested yet. |
| D13 | 2026-10-11 | The map shows a percentage for each square. This replaces "never show a chance of finding porcini" in D11. | Decided by the project owner after reading the Epic 1 report: a map without a percentage is not useful to users. Epic 1 measured no percentage that can be shown as a chance of a find. Finds only record where people looked, and the PRD says a score is not a chance until it is checked against outcomes that can be observed. The work that builds the percentage must say what it measures and check it on data that records days without porcini, such as the Bielefeld counts. |
| D14 | 2026-10-11 | The background map uses OpenStreetMap data, which is free. | Decided by the project owner. The tile server of openstreetmap.org forbids heavy use by apps, so the map images must come from a free service built on OpenStreetMap data that allows this use, such as OpenFreeMap used in P5, or from our own file of OpenStreetMap data. |
| D15 | 2026-10-11 | Prepare weather on a schedule. A background run each night fetches the weather of the weather cells that hold forest squares and stores it. Visitors read stored weather and never start a provider request. Since #100 the map, overview and details read only the published Tuscany run and a shared database cache. The window is the 30 completed Italian days before today, today and the next 7 days, 38 days in total. A day is stored once from the historical source and then reused without a time limit. A forecast does not become a completed day when its date passes, so the new completed day is fetched from the historical source. A failed request is never stored as a gap, and a failed run keeps the last stored weather and estimates with their real date. Only an explicit correction creates a new history version, and the older rows stay in the database. The national goal stays. The current code runs the schedule for Tuscany only. Changed on 13 October 2026. This replaces fetching on the first visitor request of a day, which replaced "one automatic refresh each day" in the product PRD. | Decided by the project owner. The first version fetched weather where visitors looked. The owner now wants Tuscany weather prepared in the background and saved locally. Tuscany needs 1,332 counted calls for the first load and 666 for a normal night, inside the allowance of 540 per minute, 4,500 per hour, 9,000 per day and 270,000 per 31 days. Batches hold at most 100 places and the run starts one request at a time, at least 90 seconds apart. A run on 11 October 2026 sent 3,726 places in 38 requests of up to 100 places. Open-Meteo refused 4 of those requests as too many, so requests must wait and retry. |
| D16 | 2026-10-11 | Weather cells are 0.1 degrees, about 11 by 8 km. Every forest square uses the weather of the cell that contains it. | On 11 October 2026, the rain of the last 7 days at 0.1 degree points inside one 0.25 degree cell differed by up to 39 mm from the cell centre in five mountain areas, for example 7 to 46 mm in Trentino. That is more than the 30 mm of the rain rule. The 3,726 cells with forest fit the free allowance at one counted call each a day. |
| D17 | 2026-10-11 | Only 500 m squares with at least 10 percent forest in the Copernicus forest map get estimates, and only weather cells that contain such squares get weather. Other squares show that they have no forest. | Porcini grow with trees, and 10 percent keeps small woods of about 2.5 hectares. Sampled every 100 m on 11 October 2026, Italy has 713,173 such squares out of 1,202,803, in 3,726 of its 3,802 weather cells. |
| D18 | 2026-10-11 | Keep the weather of the 21 previous days, today and the next 7 days for each weather cell. The method looks at rain from 21 to 8 days before the target day, rain of the last 7 days and the weather of the target day. Visitors see rain totals for 7, 14 and 21 days. This replaces the 30-day windows of D8 and the product PRD. | Decided by the project owner. The Regione Piemonte sheet for *B. edulis* says porcini come up 12 to 15 days after at least 30 mm of rain. In P4, rain of the last 7 days alone ranked find days no better than a random ranking. |
| D19 | 2026-10-13 | Show mushroom scores at every zoom. Wide views use averages of stored forest-square scores in larger areas. Local views show individual 500 m squares. Missing scores remain marked as missing. | Owner revision. Zoom changes detail, not the data type. Overview reads never trigger weather downloads. |
| D20 | 2026-10-11 | Store estimates as one row per forest square holding all 8 dates. Keep only the current and the previous estimates of each area and daily weather values, not hourly ones. | One row per square and date would need about 400 MB per day of estimates instead of about 50 MB. The app shows only today and the next 7 days, so nothing older is needed. The estimated total is about 300 MB and does not grow. |
| D21 | 2026-10-13 | Reassess the rules using the owner's document on ideal porcini conditions. Use 30 days of weather history where needed, replacing the 21-day history limit in D18. Keep habitat suitability and current fruiting conditions internal and show one final score. Preserve the versions and explanations of saved estimates. | Decided by the project owner during #91. The document describes moisture retained after rainfall, drying, temperature bands and compatible habitat. Its approximate ranges do not specify universal coefficients. Record additional numerical choices explicitly. The investigation, v4 implementation and remaining limits are documented in `reports/mvp/pistoia_formula_reassessment.md`. |
| D22 | 2026-10-14 | The first version covers Tuscany, region 09, only. Other Italian regions come later, and the landing page says they are coming soon. Outside Tuscany the map and area details say why no estimate exists and show no percentage. All-Italy coverage stays the goal for a later version, and the national habitat builds and Italy planning figures stay as they are. This replaces the launch commitment of "at least all Italy" in the product PRD. | Decided by the project owner during #61. #96 prepared and published Tuscany estimates from local data. Italy would need 14,904 counted calls for a first load and 7,452 each night, against 1,092 and 666 for Tuscany, as recorded in `reports/mvp/tuscany_acceptance.md`. |
| D23 | 2026-10-16 | Take all weather from the Open-Meteo `ecmwf_ifs` files in the public AWS bucket: the 30 past days, today and the 7 days ahead. The nightly run makes no Open-Meteo API call in its normal path. The weather already stored in production is rebuilt from the files, and the Tuscany estimates are published again. This replaces the Open-Meteo API as the weather source of D15. | Decided by the project owner after #127. For past days the files and the API give the same rain and soil moisture in all 333 Tuscany cells. For the days ahead the API's default mix takes rain and temperature first from the forecasts of the German weather service, and the files hold the forecast of the European weather centre, ECMWF. On 16 October this moved the estimates of days 3 to 7 by a typical 7 to 12 points, in a week when the two forecasts disagreed on a storm. The owner prefers one free source without API limits over matching the API's mix. What is lost is the 2 km German forecast of the first 2 days, which changed no estimate by more than 8 points on 16 October. A check of both forecasts against the rain of that storm, #145, was dropped, because one storm cannot show which forecast is better. See `reports/bulk_weather_estimates.md`. |
| D24 | 2026-10-18 | The map covers all 20 Italian regions, ISTAT codes 01 to 20. The landing page and the map no longer say that other regions are coming soon. A place with no published estimate still says why and shows no percentage. This replaces "Tuscany, region 09, only" in D22. | Decided by the project owner after #154 and #155. The nightly run for all Italy fits the limits of the VPS, as recorded in `docs/weather_updates.md` and `reports/italy_night_cost.md`. |
